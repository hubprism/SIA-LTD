Climate & Resilience
August 2026

From Climate Commitments to Climate Resilience

Climate ambition is no longer simply about setting targets. Organisations increasingly need to understand how climate risks affect their operations, investments, communities and long-term development objectives.

Building resilience begins with understanding the risks that matter most. Physical climate risks, changing weather patterns, infrastructure exposure and vulnerable communities can all affect the success of a programme or investment.

Effective resilience therefore requires more than a policy statement. It requires practical risk assessment, adaptation planning, appropriate financing and systems for measuring whether interventions are actually reducing vulnerability.

The organisations that will be best positioned for the future are those that translate climate ambition into decisions, investments and measurable action.

ESG & Net-Zero
August 2026

Why ESG Strategy Must Move Beyond Reporting

ESG reporting is important, but reporting alone does not create sustainable performance.

Organisations need to connect ESG commitments with business strategy, operational decisions and measurable outcomes.

A credible ESG approach should identify material risks, establish clear responsibilities and create a practical roadmap for improvement.

Environmental Health
July 2026

Environmental Health as a Development Priority

Environmental conditions have a direct relationship with community wellbeing, productivity and sustainable development.

Environmental health considerations should be integrated into project planning rather than treated as an afterthought.

Strong assessments help organisations identify potential impacts, understand exposure pathways and establish practical mitigation measures.

Monitoring & Evaluation
July 2026

Evidence Is What Turns Programmes Into Results

A programme cannot demonstrate meaningful impact without reliable evidence about what changed, why it changed and who benefited.

Monitoring and evaluation should therefore be built into programme design from the beginning.

Clear indicators, appropriate data collection, learning mechanisms and independent evaluation can help organisations make better decisions throughout the programme lifecycle.

Programme Management
June 2026

The Difference Between a Good Programme and a Delivered Programme

Strong programme design matters, but successful delivery requires disciplined implementation.

Clear governance, accountability, coordination, risk management and continuous learning are all essential to keeping complex programmes on track.

Delivery is where strategy meets reality. The strongest programmes create systems that allow teams to respond to challenges without losing sight of the intended outcomes.

Training
June 2026

Building Capacity That Lasts Beyond the Engagement

Effective training should leave organisations with stronger systems, stronger people and the confidence to continue improving after the engagement ends.

Capacity building works best when learning is connected to real organisational challenges and supported by practical tools.

The objective should not simply be to complete a workshop, but to create knowledge and capabilities that continue delivering value over time.
